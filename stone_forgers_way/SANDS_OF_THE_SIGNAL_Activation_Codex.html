Before You Begin

What You Are Holding

This is not a component library. It is not a theme file. It is not a collection of presets to copy and paste.

This is a design system with a philosophy — a set of principles, visual rules, and typographic behaviors that together define a specific kind of digital world. You are about to learn how to build inside that world, or how to bring its qualities into a world you are already building.

The system has two parts. The first is an environmental design language called The Sands of the Signal, which defines how space, light, color, and architecture should feel. The second is a typographic engine called STEAM SANS, which defines how text should behave — not just look, but behave — as a living part of the environment.

You do not need any prior knowledge of either. This document will teach you both by showing you, not just telling you. Every concept is demonstrated live on this page before you are asked to implement it.

The discipline of a propagator is this: you do not simply apply a style. You understand why the style exists, and then you carry that understanding into every environment you build — whether or not the original assets are present.

Scroll through this document in order. By the end, you will have encountered every major concept, tuned the typographic engine with your own hands, and understood the principle that holds the entire system together.

Section 01

The Core Philosophy: Silicon Erosion

Every design system has a material it is made of. This one is made of sand.

Not sand as decoration. Sand as a philosophical foundation. Before you choose a color or a font weight, you must understand what sand means in this world. Sand is the material that connects the ancient and the digital — it is the hourglass that measures time, the friction that shapes stone, and the silicon that becomes the glass of your screen.

This concept is called Silicon Erosion. It is the central tension of the system: the ancient tactile history of stone and desert meeting the luminous immediacy of digital light. Every visual decision in this system exists to serve that tension.

Truth 01
Sand is Time
The ancient hourglass. The accumulation of moments. In this world, surfaces carry history — they are worn, porous, and layered with the evidence of time passing.
Truth 02
Sand is Friction
The abrasion that shapes character. Nothing in this world is perfectly smooth. Edges are worn. Transitions are gradual. The world resists, and that resistance gives it texture.
Truth 03
Sand is Silicon
The raw material of the digital world. The glass of the screen you are reading this on was once sand. The digital and the physical share the same origin. This system makes that visible.

When you build with this system, you are building a space where these three truths are always present. The environment is never static. It is always either eroding or forming — never finished, never empty.

Section 03

STEAM SANS: Typography as Environment

In most design systems, typography is a layer on top of the world. You build the environment, then you label it.

In this system, typography is the environment. Text is made of the same silicon and light as the architecture. It erodes, forms, drifts, and dissolves just as the world does. A word on a wall is not a sign; it is a piece of the wall that has learned to mean something.

The typographic engine that makes this possible is called STEAM SANS. It is not a single font. It is a system of three behavioral states — called Registers — that you choose between based on the stability of the signal the text is carrying.

The central question of STEAM SANS is not "what font should I use?" It is: "how stable is the signal at this moment in the world?"

The three registers exist on a single axis called the Stability Axis, ranging from 0 (completely resolved and rigid) to 100 (completely dissolved and scattered). The sections that follow will show you each register live, explain when to use it, and give you the code to implement it.

Register 01 Steam Sans ∴ Harris — Instrumental STABILITY 0 — RESOLVED

Harris is the most stable form of the signal. It is text that has fully resolved — it knows exactly what it is, and it holds that form without wavering. Use it for critical data, structural markers, navigation, and any moment where the user needs absolute clarity.

Try it: Type in the field below. Watch how the text holds its form completely — no drift, no blur, no animation. This is the discipline of Harris.

/* Register 01: Harris — Instrumental
   Use for: navigation, critical data, structural labels
   Stability Axis: 0 (fully resolved, no animation) */
.steam-sans-harris {
  font-family: 'DM Sans', sans-serif;
  font-weight: 300;
  letter-spacing: 0.12em;
  text-transform: uppercase;
  color: var(--near-white);
  opacity: 1;
  /* No animation. Harris is resolved. */
}
Register 02 Steam Sans ∴ HBA — Witness STABILITY 50 — DRIFTING

HBA is the signal in a state of organic observation. It is not unstable — it is alive. Each character breathes independently, drifting slightly on a slow sine wave, scaling and blurring at the peak of its breath. The effect is subtle but unmistakable: the text feels like it is trying to hold its form.

Use HBA for narrative text, environmental dialogue, ambient information, and any moment where the user is meant to feel the world around the words, not just read them.

Try it: Type in the field below. Notice how each character animates with a slightly different timing — this is intentional. The organic variation prevents the text from feeling mechanical.

Edit text → 
/* Register 02: HBA — Witness
   Use for: narrative text, ambient dialogue, lore
   Stability Axis: 50 (drifting, breathing, organic)
   Each character is wrapped in a span by JavaScript */
.hba-char {
  display: inline-block;
  animation: hba-breathe var(--dur) ease-in-out infinite;
  animation-delay: var(--del);
}
/* JS sets --dur and --del per character for organic variation */
@keyframes hba-breathe {
  0%   { transform: translateY(0) scaleY(1);    opacity: 0.88; }
  50%  { transform: translateY(-2px) scaleY(1.02); opacity: 0.93; filter: blur(0.3px); }
  100% { transform: translateY(0) scaleY(1);    opacity: 0.88; }
}
Register 03 Steam Sans ∴ Vapor — Transmission STABILITY 100 — DISSOLVING

Vapor is the signal returning to the environment. Text is no longer a stable form — it is a temporary gathering of particles that have agreed, briefly, to mean something. When the agreement ends, they scatter back into the sand.

Use Vapor for ephemeral messages, transitions, moments of high entropy, and any situation where the act of reading should feel like catching something before it disappears.

Try it: Type a word, then press DISSOLVE to watch it return to sand. Press FORM to call it back.

/* Register 03: Vapor — Transmission
   Use for: transitions, ephemeral messages, atmospheric text
   Stability Axis: 100 (fully dissolved, particle-based)
   
   Technique: render text to hidden canvas → extract pixel data
   → animate each visible pixel as an independent particle */
// 1. Render text to offscreen canvas
offCtx.font = '300 120px DM Sans';
offCtx.fillText(text, offsetX, offsetY);
// 2. Sample visible pixels as particle home positions
for (let y = 0; y < H; y += gap) {
  for (let x = 0; x < W; x += gap) {
    if (pixelData[(y * W + x) * 4 + 3] > 128) {
      particles.push({ homeX: x, homeY: y, /* … */ });
    }
  }
}
// 3. form() → move particles to home; dissolve() → scatter them

Section 07

The Stability Axis

The three registers are not separate tools. They are three points on a single continuous axis. Understanding this axis is the key to using STEAM SANS as a living system rather than a collection of styles.

The axis runs from 0 (Harris — fully resolved) through 50 (HBA — drifting) to 100 (Vapor — dissolved). In a future variable font implementation, all three states will collapse into a single CSS property. For now, you choose the register that matches the stability of the signal in your environment.

/* The future: when Steam Sans Atlas ships as a variable font,
   all three registers collapse into a single CSS rule.
   The axis values below are the design targets. */
.steam-sans-atlas {
  font-family: 'Steam Sans Atlas', 'DM Sans', sans-serif;
  font-variation-settings:
    'STBL' 50,   /* Stability: 0=Harris, 100=Vapor */
    'PRSS' 25,   /* Pressure: stroke density */
    'COHR' 100,  /* Coherence: legibility */
    'DRFT' 10;   /* Drift: baseline instability */
  transition: font-variation-settings 0.8s ease-out;
}

Section 08

Dynamical Tuning: The Developer's Inquiry

STEAM SANS is not a static font file you drop into a project and forget. It is an engine that must be tuned to match the personality of the environment you are building. The tuning happens across four vectors.

Before you set any values, answer the three questions below. Your answers will tell you where to set each slider. The live preview at the bottom of this section will update as you tune.

Question 1: What is the emotional state of the environment? Is the space calm, resolved, and functional — like a control room or a data terminal? Or is it turbulent, atmospheric, and eroding — like a desert at dusk or a signal fading at the edge of range? Calm environments lean toward low STBL (Harris). Turbulent environments lean toward high STBL (Vapor).
Question 2: Is the text meant to be read or felt? Critical instructions, navigation labels, and data readouts must be read accurately — keep COHR high. Atmospheric lore, environmental poetry, and ambient signals can be felt rather than read — COHR can drop, allowing the text to blur and scatter.
Question 3: What is the user's relationship to the signal? Are they actively forging it — in control, precise, resolved? (Harris) Are they witnessing it — present but not in control, observing the world? (HBA) Or are they losing it — at the edge of range, the signal breaking apart? (Vapor)
AXIS 01
Stability (STBL)
0 = Harris (Rigid) → 100 = Vapor (Dissolved)
0
Controls the overall entropy of the text. At 0, text is fully resolved. At 100, it is fully scattered. The register you choose should match this value.
AXIS 02
Pressure (PRSS)
0 = Light as air → 100 = Carved in stone
300
Controls stroke density and weight. High pressure feels carved into stone. Low pressure feels like light passing through glass.
AXIS 03
Coherence (COHR)
0 = Abstracted → 100 = Fully legible
100
Controls legibility. As Coherence drops, text loses contrast and clarity. Use high Coherence for functional text; lower it for atmospheric or ambient text.
AXIS 04
Drift (DRFT)
0 = Stable baseline → 100 = Characters float
0
Controls baseline instability. At high Drift, characters float independently of one another. At 0, the baseline is perfectly stable.

Section 09

The Sacred Alphabet

Beyond the three registers, this system uses a small set of special marks called the Sacred Alphabet. These are not decorative. Each mark carries a specific meaning within the world and should be used deliberately, not as punctuation.

Think of these as environmental glyphs — marks that appear on walls, in data streams, at the boundaries between spaces. When a user encounters them, they should feel like they have found something that belongs to the world, not something the designer placed there.

∴
Therefore Mark
Primary symbol of transformation. Used as a separator, a threshold marker, or a moment of consequence.
~
Flow Mark
Indicates continuity or breath. Should always be animated with the HBA breathing rhythm when used in the environment.
…
Extended Ellipsis
Represents an extended hold — a pause longer than standard. Render with extreme letter-spacing.
⟂
Boundary Mark
Marks areas of high tension or transition between environmental layers. Use at thresholds.
◌
Void Glyph
Represents absence and potential. Used sparingly, often floating in Vapor mode. Never use it to fill space.
